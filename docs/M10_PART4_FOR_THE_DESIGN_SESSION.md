# Lab 10, Part 4 (Nights at the Logs): what changed on the walk, and what is still wrong with it

Written 2026-10-27 by the build session, for the session that wrote `m10_memory_SCENE_SPEC.md`, so that the design can be revisited with what the walk found. The lab lives on branch `m10-memory` of `~/Documents/Projects/bcogapp`; the as-built notes are `docs/M10_AS_BUILT_NOTES.md` (§2.4 the clearings and nights, §4.1 to §4.4 every measured number, §7 every departure from the spec); the walk-through, with Jon's comments and answers, is `LAB_10_WALKTHROUGH.md` beside the spec. ## 1. The part as it stands

The student is the small mammal of Lab 9, in its range at night, in the eye or follow view (follow by default), driven by the keys. A clearing has ten logs 4 to 11 m from a nest, seven with loose bark and three smooth, and three or four landmarks. A dig takes 3 s and shows whether beetles are there; a meal 3 s more. Halfway through each night the young call; digging stops until the student has gone back to the nest and nursed (8 s, by itself at the hole's rim); at the night's end the animal walks home by itself while the light rises. Two rules are fixed on screen: loose-bark logs have beetles unless recently eaten, smooth logs never do; a log emptied tonight is empty the next night and full the night after.

A practice night (120 s, unscored, may be redone) in the clearing around Lab 9's nest, then two blocks of three nights (90 s each) in two other clearings, one block of look-alike nights (same moon, weather, thing on the ground, sound on all three) and one of distinct nights (each differs on every feature), the order by seed. After each night, with no warning before the first, the map question: the clearing from above with the nest, the landmarks and the logs numbered by kind, one answer per loose-bark log (emptied tonight / last night / two nights ago / not emptied, the choices growing with the night), nothing shown of what happened. Before the second block the student commits a prediction: compared with the first three nights, will confusing which night a log was emptied be more, less or about as common. The reveal, only after both blocks: per night the tallies (beetles, digs at full logs, digs at logs emptied last night, full logs passed over that were emptied two nights ago, repeat digs before and after the call, digs at smooth logs), the overhead replay with the path and each dig by outcome, the night's confusion table (what was true against what was marked; the off-diagonal night cells are confusions between nights); per block the summed table; the prediction and what happened. Part 5 runs programmed foragers (Usual only, Tendency only, Tonight too, and Tonight too with the nights blurred) on nights like the student's and shows their tallies and tables beside the student's.

## 2. What changed from the spec, and why

In the order the changes were made. Items marked *build* were the build session's decisions before the walk, recorded in the notes' §7; items marked *walk* are Jon's, 2026-10-27.

1. *build.* **The night was 55 s, not the spec's "about 2 minutes"** (§8.3), because §14 asked for a length at which a scripted perfect rememberer empties about four of seven loose-bark logs, and at 2 minutes it empties all seven. The length was swept (notes §4.1).
2. *build.* **§14's second target holds on night 2 only.** A scripted forager that knows only the usual fact digs at about two logs emptied the night before on night 2 but about one on night 3, at any length, because a forager digging at random empties about two logs on night 2, so only about two are "emptied last night" on night 3. Reported, not retuned.
3. *build.* **Distinct nights share at most one feature value with the look-alike nights, never the thing on the ground** (§8.4 and §14 said none). With three moons, three weathers and three sounds, three nights that differ on every feature cannot all avoid the look-alike value on each. A fourth value of each was not added.
4. *build.* **The practice clearing is Lab 9's nest with Lab 9's landmarks**, with its own geometry; the spec's four clearings shared one geometry. Jon's judgment call on keeping it is still open (walk-through §9, item 5).
5. *build.* **Usual only chooses at random among loose-bark logs**, not nearest first, since nearest-first would redig the logs it emptied the night before as surely as Tendency only does and the two would not separate.
6. *walk.* **The practice night is 120 s**, longer than the scored nights, for learning the keys: at 55 s Jon ate about one beetle while learning them.
7. *walk.* **A scored night is 90 s**, with the call at 45 s. Jon, after his first block: the call at the half, the walk back and the nursing left no time to reach a log again; the nights were too short to be meaningful; the time came back from the memory experiments, which were shortened earlier on the walk (Part 1 is seven lists of fifteen words, not eleven of two lengths).
8. *walk, the build's remedy, accepted by Jon.* **The scripted foragers walk at half the key pace (0.75 m/s).** At 90 s and the key pace every scripted forager had time to dig all seven logs, so memory stopped paying for them: Usual only ate as many beetles over a block as Tonight too (11.8 against 11.9), Tendency only dug at fewer emptied logs than Usual only on night 2, and the store's medium setting fell under the test's floor. At half the key pace, which stands for the searching and turning a student does between logs, a 90 s night gives the perfect forager 4.6 / 2.2 / 4.6 of seven and Usual only 1.7 digs at emptied logs on night 2, so §14's targets hold again and Part 5's contrasts stand. Jon noted a possible later change toward a more human scripted walk, such as random movements in the wrong direction that take time.
9. *walk.* **The interface**: the call, the nursing and the dawn get a large colored line over the view; the night's countdown is large over the view; three pups lie in the nest and squirm while nursed; the rat acts out the dig, the meal and the nursing in the follow view; the follow view is the default; the energy bar is labeled, since it read as a night timer; the practice night can be redone from the first block's screen.
10. *walk, bugs.* Each drawn log was the mirror image of the line the body is stopped at (a sign error), so students were blocked by logs that were not there and could not reach the ones they saw; the root over the nest could be walked through, then was made solid along its whole span, and is now solid at its two feet only; the nest is reached at the hole's rim (0.5 m), not 0.8 m out; the fallen branch stood on end with no footprint and now lies down with one. These moved the scripted foragers' numbers by a tenth or two; everything in notes §4 was re-measured.

Not changed, and tested: a log never shows whether it has beetles until it is dug; no minimap, mark or trail is drawn while a night runs; the first map question comes with no string before it that mentioned it; the refill rule runs exactly as stated; the reveal opens only after both blocks.

## 4. The practice confound (Jon, 2026-10-27), and the build session's thoughts

**The problem.** The second block comes with practice, and the task is hard without it. The practice effect pushes confusions down in the second block whatever its kind. A student who gets the look-alike block second has practice pulling confusions down while the context pushes them up, so the two cancel and the effect may vanish or reverse. A student who gets the distinct block second has both pulling the same way and sees a huge effect. Jon's expectation is that the practice effect swamps the context effect within a student, that students with distinct nights first may reverse the predicted effect, and that students with look-alike nights first may detect the confound. The seed balances the order across students, which is a between-student test that no single student can see, since results stay in the browser.

**What drives the practice effect, specifically.** Three things, which pull apart: (a) navigation, which the longer night and the map after night 1 already ease; (b) the dig count, which rises with practice and raises the number of answers at stake, so raw confusion counts rise with it; (c) the encoding strategy, which is naive on night 1 and deliberate afterward, and which the map-first to-do would make deliberate from the start.

**Options, roughly from cheapest to most invasive.**

1. **Count confusions as a share, not a count.** The reveal shows confusion counts; Part 5 already reports the foragers' confusions as a share of the logs at stake. Showing the student's as a share removes the part of the practice effect that comes from digging more (b), not the rest.
2. **Flatten the practice curve before the scored blocks.** A practice block instead of a practice night: three nights with the map question each, in the practice clearing, unscored (or scored as a baseline the reveal shows). With the map shown first (to-do 1) the student reaches the scored blocks already knowing the task, the keys, and the question. Cost about six minutes at 90 s a night. This is the one the build session would try first; it attacks (a), (b) and (c) at once and needs no new mechanism.
3. **Make the confound the lesson.** The handout has the student state their order and their two confusion counts (or shares); pairs with the opposite order compare; or a shared class sheet gives the order-by-kind table, which is the between-student test the seed was for. Students who detect the confound have learned something worth more than the effect. Part 5 helps here: the programmed foragers have no practice effect, so their look-alike against distinct contrast is the pure context effect, and the student's own data can be set against it as "behavior with practice in it" against "a model without".
4. **A third, interleaved design.** Instead of two blocks, six nights alternating kinds is impossible, since look-alike means three nights in a row; but two short blocks of two nights each, run twice (ABAB or ABBA by seed), would put practice into both kinds within a student. Cost: more nights, more maps, a weaker per-block memory load (two nights give only "tonight" and "last night", so the "two nights ago" confusion disappears). Probably not worth it.
5. **Lower the memory load so context matters more than being overwhelmed.** Fewer loose-bark logs (five instead of seven), or fewer logs in all. The spec's seven is also what makes the "about four of seven" tuning meaningful; changing it reopens the tuning.
6. **Report the prediction against the balanced class, not the self.** Keep the within-student reveal but word the prediction and the handout's question as "for a student who did the blocks in the other order" as well as for oneself, so the student reasons about the confound before seeing the data.

**Ideas the build left out or that the walk raised, for the design session.**

- A more human scripted walker (Jon): random movements in the wrong direction that take time, so that the scripted foragers' nights resemble a student's rather than a straight-line walker at half pace.
- The four clearings share one geometry turned and mirrored so that blocks are matched; the practice clearing does not. If a practice block is added (option 2), it could use the shared geometry too, or keep Lab 9's landmarks as a familiar place to learn in.
- The map after each night is also the only reference frame by which a log can be named, since the logs carry no marks in the world. Any design that drops the overview must find another way to ask "which log".
- The confusion table can only count confusions for logs the student actually emptied; a student who empties two logs a night gives two answers at stake, and the kinds of night cannot differ on them. The length and the pace were set so that a student has time for about four; whether students at the keys reach that is still unmeasured (Jon, learning the keys, emptied about one in 55 s).
- A fourth value of each feature (a fourth moon state, weather, sound) would let distinct nights avoid the look-alike values entirely (§8.4 as written). Not done; recorded.
