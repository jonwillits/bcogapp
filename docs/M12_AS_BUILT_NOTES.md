## 1. Every control and screen, as it shipped

The header panel, titled **Lab 12: Intelligence and Thinking Ahead**, is on every tab: the five tabs with their part under the name (**Open, Choices, Puzzles, Band, Corn**), the saved-here line (or the storage-unavailable line), and the **Run seed** (drawn when the saved state is first made, changed only by **Clear my saved results**); on the Band tab also **View** (**Eye view** / **Follow view**), **Narrower view** and **Low detail**. The layout is Lab 10's: a full-bleed view with floating panels, the header and the part's controls on the left, the part's results or instruments on the right, and what a part shows in the middle in a panel centered over the view; on the Band tab the range is drawn in the view itself and the middle holds only a thin status strip. **Every part, and every step within a part, opens on an instruction screen** (Lab 10's `progress/InstructionScreen.tsx`): the title, *What you will do*, *How long*, *Pausing*, *What you need*, *The keys and buttons*, the part's content lines, and **Begin**. **No tab is ever locked** (decision 7); `?dev=1` is accepted for symmetry with the other labs and changes nothing. Lab 9's conventions hold: a button's or a key's name in running text is in quotes, a block of numbers is a block of aligned rows, a selected control changes color and never size, every text is selectable, no browser dialog anywhere.

**The five procedures on the puzzle:**


- Search everything: It examines arrangements in order of their distance from the start, in moves. It takes the arrangement that has waited longest, lists every arrangement one legal move away that it has not seen, adds each to the end of its waiting list, and goes on until the arrangement it takes is the goal. Then it reads back the moves that led there. Stores every arrangement waiting to be examined, and for every arrangement seen, which arrangement it was reached from. Source: §12.2.2 (search), computed.

- Goal recursion: To move a stack of k disks from one peg to another, it sets three goals in order: move the top k − 1 disks to the spare peg, move the largest of the k disks, then move the k − 1 disks back on top of it. Each goal to move a stack is met the same way, so the goals nest, and a goal to move one disk is a move. Stores a stack of goals, which grows to depth n in solving an n-disk problem. Source: simon_1975 pp. 269–270, 279.

- Largest disk out of place: It looks at the pegs, finds the largest disk that is not yet on the right peg, and finds the largest disk in that disk’s way: on top of it, or on the right peg. If nothing is in the way, it moves the disk. Otherwise its goal becomes moving the disk in the way to the third peg, and it looks again from there. This is means–ends analysis (§12.2.2): a heuristic, a rule of thumb that directs search toward small, promising parts of a problem space, which chooses each move by the difference between the current state and the goal and makes the conditions for a move that cannot yet be made into a subgoal. Stores one current goal, whatever the number of disks. Source: simon_1975 pp. 272, 281.

- Move pattern: On every odd-numbered move it moves the smallest disk one peg along, always in the same direction around the three pegs. The direction depends on whether the number of disks is odd or even. On every even-numbered move it makes the one legal move that does not move the smallest disk. Stores whether the next move is odd or even. Source: simon_1975 p. 273 (its page says “pegs” where it means disks; p. 282 says disks).

- Memorized moves: It has the 15 moves of the four-disk solution as a list, each move a peg to take from and a peg to put on, and applies them in order. Stores the list of 15 moves. Source: simon_1975 pp. 272, 287.


**The three lookers:**


- Look at nothing: It uncovers no number and takes one of the eight paths at random.

- Look at everything: It uncovers all 14 numbers, at a cost of 14 points, and takes the path with the highest sum.

- One more look when it is worth it: For every number still hidden, it computes how much uncovering that one number is expected to raise the value of the best path, taking each hidden number to be −10, −5, +5 or +10 with equal chance. If the largest such gain is more than the 1-point cost of a look, it uncovers that number and computes again. Otherwise it stops and takes the path whose expected sum is highest.

## 3. The models, as they shipped

**The bisections** halve an interval six times from its middle; the point of indifference is the midpoint of the last interval; within $100/64 of a scripted chooser's. **The fits** are least squares over the five points, a grid of 400 over γ in [0.9, 1] or k in [0, 1] then a golden-section refinement. **The best policies** are exact over policies "wait up to t seconds then take" for t from 0 to 20 in halves and never taking: blue, always wait, 33.33 points a minute; orange, wait 8 s then take, 16.36; taking at once 12.00 at either. **The survival table** is exact. **The procedures** are `procedures.ts`, as §1a describes them; the memorized list is goal recursion's 15 moves on four disks. **The myopic looker** computes, for each hidden node, the mean over its four possible values of the best expected path value once known minus the best now, under a prior of −10, −5, +5, +10 at equal chance; it looks while the largest gain exceeds 1 and takes the path with the highest expected sum (ties by the seed). **The value learner** (`hominins/valueLearner.ts`) is Lab 5's Verdict setting: one actor unit per errand (fetch a cobble, fetch a sapling, make a flake, make a probe, make a digging stick, go to each of the eight places, stay) and one critic, each over 1,024 inputs, one per state a hominin can stand in (4 cues × 32 hand codes × 8 places); η 0.3, γ 0.9 per errand, a trace of 0.8 per errand, weights within Lab 5's ±3; δ = γV(after) − V(before) at each errand and r − V(final) at midday, released through `thirdFactor('verdict')` to the marked connections and written by `learnWeights`; the actor chooses by a softmax at temperature 0.3 over the errands available. **The planners' model** (`hominins/model.ts`) is built from observations alone: which places yielded which items, what each make used and kept and where it was seen, mean walking and act seconds, and the chance of food by cue, place and item in hand, moved by α = 0.5 toward each outcome seen (the elder's and its own); the Shallow planner's D is 2 and the Planner's is unbounded (capped at 6 for the search); the value is the chance of food at the end, or, in variant B when that is none, the critic's estimate of the end state; ## 8. Where the build departed from the spec, or where its expectations turned out wrong

1. **A take at a dispenser pauses 5 s, not 1 s** (§5.4). With a 1-s pause after a take, taking 1 point at once every trial earns 60 points a minute against 33 for waiting for 5 every 9 s, so never waiting would be the best policy at both dispensers and the spec's stated best policies would be false. With 5 s, waiting is best at the blue (33.3 against 12.0) and waiting 8 s then taking is best at the orange (16.4 against 12.0), as the spec says; the instruction screen states both pauses without saying when a dispenser delivers.
2. **The value learner's features are one per state, not a sum over cue, item and place** (§7.4.3 names what it learns about, not the coding). A first build summed one weight per cue, per item and per place; the sum credited *go to the grassland* under vultures with the value of going there holding a flake, and in some seeds Learned value walked to the grassland empty-handed (T1 0.78 with a spread of 0.27) or to the old kill under vultures. With one feature per state T1 is 0.90 and T3 0.87. The actor chooses by a softmax (temperature 0.3) rather than the greedy choice with ties at random, so that a falling preference shows as going less; weights stay within Lab 5's ±3 rather than ±8 (the actor's preferences had ratcheted to the ceiling because unrewarded days touch only the errands taken).
3. **The history is 500 days, not 300.** At 300, Learned value had not learned the marrow chain in every seed (T3 0.75, one seed at 0.0); at 500, 0.87 with no seed under 0.5. The deviation days are a quarter termites and tubers, which the elder uses up, so the item-5 condition holds by construction and is tested on the printed counts.
4. **The planners stop going to the empty grassland after four or five days, not after day 1** (§7.4.4's expected T2 row). Their chance of food at (vultures, the grassland, a flake) moves by α = 0.5 toward each empty day, from about 1 to 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 and 0.06, and a plan is worth going for above 0.1, so the strip falls on day 5 and reaches near zero by day 7. A model of 500 days overturned by one morning would need α near 1; the two numbers are the knob, and the walk-through asks.
5. **Learned value does not visibly slow on T2 within ten days.** Its preference for the chain stands at the ceiling after 500 days, and ten unrewarded mornings at η 0.3 take it down by about 3, to zero only at the end of the run, so the softmax still chooses the chain on 0.8 to 0.95 of days (0.79 at 300 days of history, 0.89 at 500). The spec's "goes less, over several days" would show in a longer run or at a higher rate.
6. **Variant B equals variant A everywhere but the Shallow planner's T2**, where B keeps going to the grassland longer and oscillates (0.59; its strip 0.85 0.85 0.40 0.65 0.10 0.25 0.35 0.80 1.00 0.95): the critic's value of the grassland state falls only on days it is visited, and when other end states have fallen further the grassland wins again. The spec's "may partly pass T5" did not happen: the critic's values are one per state and do not reach the dry cue's states, which it never stood in with a flake. Jon's choice of the default is between a Shallow planner that stops as the Planner does (A) and one that keeps returning to a place its past valued (B).
7. **The myopic looker never looks below a −40.** Under its prior (−10, −5, +5, +10), the best path through a −40 can gain at most 20 more, so no further look below it is worth a point, and it misses the +60 on the three trees whose best path runs through the loss (about 30 points a student), while earning the most net points of the three (94 against 38 for looking at everything and −82 for looking at nothing). It prunes as the reading says people do, for a different reason: not a reflex to the loss but a prior that has no +60 in it. The reveal states this beside the student's own share.
8. **Pairs 3 and 4 are Problems 7/7′ and 8/8′ of 1979**, gamble against gamble rather than sure against gamble, because those are the reflection pairs Ruggeri et al. replicated beyond pair 1 (their items 5/9 in every country, 6/10 in 84%); the amounts are the 1979 numbers in dollars, as pair 1's are (Ruggeri et al. doubled them for the United States), and the counted option is the more probable one.
9. **The student's day runs at 2.5 range seconds a second**, so the morning is 2.4 real minutes and the two days about 6 minutes against the spec's 5; the frame loop's clamp (0.05 s a frame) slows the range clock on a slow machine rather than skipping.
10. **The cues are visual and in text; no sound.** Hyenas "whooping" and termites "rising" are named in the status strip and drawn; nothing plays.
11. **Part 3's runs are not saved** across a reload (as Lab 11's arena); the predictions, the diagnosis and the variant setting are.
12. **The in-app matrix is 20 seeds × one run per test** for each variant (240 runs), paid one history slice or one run per timer tick; the crib's separability table used three runs per seed.
13. **The programmed hominins walk straight lines between places** replayed from their day's segments; the scattered trees are placed off every such line, and only the student's body uses Lab 10's walk around a footprint.
